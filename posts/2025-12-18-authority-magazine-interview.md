Digital transformation has become a crucial component for businesses striving to stay competitive and relevant in today's rapidly evolving landscape. As technology continues to shape industries and redefine business models, companies must adapt and leverage digital tools and strategies to unlock new opportunities for growth and innovation. In this interview series, we aim to explore various aspects of digital transformation, including best practices, challenges, success stories, and expert insights. We are talking to thought leaders, industry experts, entrepreneurs, technology innovators, and executives who have firsthand experience in driving digital transformation initiatives within their organizations. As part of this series, we had the pleasure of interviewing Alexander Alvord.

Alexander Alvord is a Solutions Architect at Nutanix and a leading voice in hybrid and multi-cloud transformation. Since joining the Global NC2 Unit in its earliest phase, he has helped scale the team from fewer than a dozen to over 250 members by 2025 — driving pilot, production, and PoC deployments for some of the largest enterprises across the Americas. With deep expertise in automation, governance, and multi-cloud strategy, Alexander simplifies IT complexity and empowers organizations to escape costly vendor lock-in.

He is a core contributor to Nutanix's Best Practice Guides, Certification Program, and Validated Designs, and serves on the elite Xpert Cloud team. His focus spans infrastructure-as-code, BCDR design, application modernization, and advanced networking. As a thought leader in the space, he also dedicates his time to global enablement — mentoring engineers, building scalable architectures, and helping customers design resilient, secure, service-oriented systems.

Beyond his enterprise work, Alexander has led the Seattle Nutanix User Group for over five years and is a passionate advocate for servant leadership, knowledge sharing, and community-building. Whether he's podcasting, mentoring, or designing cutting-edge architectures, Alexander is part of a movement disrupting the cloud status quo — helping companies regain agility, reduce hidden costs, and make the cloud work for them.

## The backstory

**Thank you so much for joining us in this interview series. Before we dive in, our readers would love to "get to know you" a bit better. Can you tell us a bit about your 'backstory' and how you got started?**

Great question. I was an undergrad and had an opportunity at that time as a general manager to implement a Point-of-Sales system, and saw our inventory age drastically reduce, freeing up cash flow to invest in other areas of the business operations. That was my "Aha" moment with technology. Early in my career as an Enterprise Architect, I saw IaaS emerging in 2007 and became passionate about the orchestration. While AWS was storming the market with EC2, I had a feeling the management of those fleets would hold the real value. I left Enterprise Architecture to chase the emerging cloud section in 2014, joining RightScale. That was significant in my career as it was the first of many — first DevOps shop, first Start-up, first RSU round, etc. The other significant part of RightScale (for those that don't know the history) was a four-pronged approach to the business. 1. RightScale orchestration — delivering multi-cloud 2. The earliest and most comprehensive cloud costing guide based on all cloud/services 3. Ability to resell the hyperscalers (Azure, Google, AWS) 4. Engineering prowess to refactor apps to be cloud-ready/native. All of this really opened my eyes to what was possible, and it was my early peek into my growing interest around cost optimization — FinOps.

**Can you share a story about the funniest mistake you made when you were first starting? Can you tell us what lessons or 'takeaways' you learned from that?**

Early in my career on the technology partner side, I began figuring out how to apply for co-op funds for upskilling. One particular clause I read led to a shipment from NetApp on "Free Technical Swag," so I am in a call center with ~1,400 people, and in walks a delivery man who took 7 trips with his dolly stacked with boxes. Let's just say the floor lost some valuable call productivity for about 30 minutes.

**None of us are able to achieve success without some help along the way. Is there a particular person who you are grateful for who helped get you to where you are? Can you share a story?**

I've been lucky, I guess; talent seeks talent. Early in college, my technology business professor saw the drive and grit in me and took me under his wing. Since college, I have advocated for building bridges and connections with senior and seasoned professionals. I've had help all along the way. One thing I have learned is this: coaching and mentoring are very different things. Who do you really want to serve? Do you want to give back to the community? In what way? These questions answered have opened so many new doors for me.

**Is there a particular book, podcast, or film that made a significant impact on you? Can you share a story or explain why it resonated with you so much?**

Who hasn't seen Hackers? It was my first look at how cool and punk rock technology could be. The film spanned gender, orientation, and ageism — it's a great film. Another one that really sticks is an older PBS Frontline United States of Secrets that tackled an incredible scale.

**Are you working on any new, exciting projects now? How do you think that might help people?**

Right now, I am incredibly busy working on many projects at the moment. One in particular I can share is modernizing my lab for a cloud-native approach. Implementing zero trust as an example is a worthwhile venture, and those skills can translate directly to what users are doing across the Enterprise.

## Digital transformation

**Thank you for all that. Let's now turn to the main focus of our discussion about Digital Transformation. Digital transformation can mean many things to many people, from your perspective, how do you define digital transformation in your industry?**

Great question. For the cloud/AI industry — it's the process of rethinking and evolving a company culture and customer engagement through the integration of digital technologies to improve operations, create new business models, and deliver exceptional experiences.

**Which companies can most benefit from a Digital Transformation?**

Anyone delivering goods and services with an e-commerce presence is a great candidate for digital transformation.

**Has integrating Digital Transformation been a challenging process for some companies? What are the challenges? How do you help resolve them?**

Yes, the challenges are everywhere across every discipline. Licensing, cost proliferation, operations, development, procurement, sovereignty, internal threats (executive turnover, legal), external threats (political, law, war, climate). These include complex subjects across multiple organizational disciplines that require alignment, investment, and refinement.

## Five ways a company can use digital transformation to take it to the next level

**Based on your experience and success, what are "Five Ways a Company Can Use Digital Transformation To Take It To The Next Level"? Please share a story or an example for each.**

1. **E-commerce/mobile app development.** Allows new channels and new customers, and new ways to reach them personally. Example: A Credit Union providing a cloud-based VDI environment can turn into new members/communities by providing services to areas once deemed too high for capital investment.
2. **Healthcare can tackle wearables and telehealth** to provide new lines of business and customer reach. Example: Healthcare provider issues a new wearable that tracks delivery of medicine and helps reduce Code Blues, freeing up operational overhead.
3. **Implement ML for data analysis and automation.** Creating a data lake and combing through that analysis can result in significant business/customer insights that can further drive campaigns and top of the line funnel.
4. **Airlines can tackle modern internet connectivity,** providing best-in-class options, resulting in a perceived premium in the brand.
5. **Implementing modern apps to have control over the entire customer value chain,** centralize ordering and delivery tracking through the app, and use analysis to provide customer loyalty. Example: Your favorite local-ish new t-shirt brand has a cardless loyalty program, resulting in 18% more sales and providing a jump in lifetime customer value.
