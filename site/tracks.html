Tracks

Music written as code and rendered from the script. Each track on this page is an original arrangement by Alex, built in Sonic Pi, the live-coding synth, and bounced to audio without a keyboard, a microphone, or anyone else's recording in the chain.

These are arrangements, not reproductions. The songs belong to their writers; the code and the render are Alex's.

  1. Three Little Birds

    Sonic Pi arrangement of the Bob Marley song · 74 bpm · 130 s · MP3, 192 kbps

    This is Alex's own arrangement, not Bob Marley's recording and not a sample of it. It is a Sonic Pi script: four live loops running against each other at 74 bpm, the classic reggae tempo. The script is what was rendered, headless, through Sonic Pi's engine, for 130 seconds.

    • Piano skank: A major and D major chords on the off-beat, the "and" of every beat.
    • Lead melody: the chorus line, played on a synth lead.
    • Drums: a one-drop reggae pattern, bass drum on the one, snare on two and four.
    • Hi-hat: closed hi-hat on steady eighths.

    Sonic Pi has no arrangement view and no timeline; the loops are the arrangement. The same script played longer would be the same song, longer. Where the lab's instrument turns infrastructure state into sound, this is the plainer case: a score in a text file, and an engine that plays it.

  2. Is This Love

    Sonic Pi arrangement of the Bob Marley song · 122 bpm · 142 s · seed 7 · MP3, 192 kbps

    This is Alex's own arrangement, not Bob Marley's recording and not a sample of it. It is a Sonic Pi script: six live loops at 122 bpm, and this one has a structure. One loop is an arrangement director that cycles the song through verse and chorus, pre-chorus, and solo, switching every 16 beats; the other loops read which section is current and play their part for it. The script was rendered headless through Sonic Pi's engine for 142 seconds, six full passes through the cycle, from random seed 7, so the solo comes out the same every time it is bounced.

    • Arrangement director: sets the section, verse and chorus, then pre-chorus, then solo, 16 beats each, and repeats.
    • FM bassline: F-sharp minor to D under the verse, chorus, and solo; a G-sharp minor to C-sharp minor walk under the pre-chorus.
    • Piano skank: the chords on the off-beat, following the same section changes.
    • Lead line: switches synths per section. A saw lead carries the chorus melody, blade pads sustain through the pre-chorus, and the solo is a plucked F-sharp minor pentatonic line, notes and timing picked at random from the fixed seed, panned across the stereo field.
    • Drums: a one-drop pattern, bass drum on the one, snare on two and four, steady through every section.
    • Hi-hat: a closed hi-hat shuffle on eighths, pitched up slightly.

    The director is the whole trick. Each loop keeps its own time and only asks one question at the top of each pass through its pattern: which section are we in. That is enough to turn six independent loops into a song with a shape.
