Diagram as Code & Diagram as Art

Two habits from the same keyboard. The first is the blueprint: the lab's own architecture drawn twice, once as an ASCII box map that survives a terminal, a ticket and an email, and once as a Mermaid flowchart that a browser renders from ten lines of text. The second is play: Japanese motifs drawn in the same eighty columns, and an orca set that borrows the blueprint's own trick — the same subject once as a picture, once as a graph. Both are text in git before they are pictures.

Rights. Every image on this page is the property of Cloud Lab Works LLC and is shown watermarked. Personal data has been removed from the blueprints (no addresses, hosts, people or account ids); what remains is the shape. Sources, licences and versions drawn for another estate are by inquiry.

The orca set: once as a picture, once as a graph

Three pieces, each drawn twice the way the blueprints above are: the ASCII on the left is the drawing, the Mermaid on the right is the same subject read as a sequence or a structure instead of a shape. Orca, not "killer whale" — the site's own rule, stated on the mark's own card.

  • ASCII · monospace, ink on cream · Mermaid flowchartShachiAn orca surfacing: the back breaks first, then the fin, and that is the whole of what the water gives you. Japanese writes the animal 鯱, the fish radical beside the tiger, a tiger being the nearest thing they had to compare it to. The graph on the right is the same four seconds read as a cycle instead of a silhouette.Inquire about the original →
  • ASCII · monospace, ink on cream · Mermaid flowchartThe podFour dorsal fins at the surface and nothing else showing. Tallest is a bull, the straight blades are cows and a calf tucked beside one of them — this is the whole of what you see from a beach. The graph on the right is what the beach doesn't show: the matriline underneath, a grandmother leading daughters, a son who never leaves.Inquire about the original →
  • ASCII · monospace, ink on cream · Mermaid flowchartSeigaihaBlue sea waves. Concentric arcs, each rank offset by half a wave, repeating until the eye gives up and reads it as water — the oldest pattern here, and the only one that tiles. The graph on the right is the rule the tiling follows, not the water itself.Inquire about the original →
How it is made. The sources are one Python file: an ASCII block and a Mermaid block per blueprint, an ASCII block per motif, and for the orca set both an ASCII block and a Mermaid block per piece. A script renders the text with a monospace face on cream, renders the Mermaid in a headless browser, pads both, and bakes the watermark in. Rerun the script and every image on this page is rebuilt from the text; nothing here was drawn with a mouse.
